Ask a stalled creator which of their promotion actually works and you will usually get a shrug or a guess — a feeling that Reddit is "probably" the big one, or that a viral clip "seemed to" help. That shrug is the whole problem. If you cannot say which post, channel, or campaign produced a subscriber, you do not have a funnel you can improve; you have a set of activities and a hope that some of them work. This guide is part of our [growth hub](/resources/growth), and it is about the unglamorous instrumentation that turns that hope into something you can actually steer.

## Attribution is the difference between a funnel and a guess

A funnel is only useful if you can *see* it — if you can trace a subscriber back to what brought them and therefore know which efforts to do more of. Without that visibility, every allocation decision you make is blind. You will either keep doing everything, pouring hours into channels that produce nothing because you cannot tell they produce nothing, or you will keep doing the loudest thing, mistaking the promotion you remember for the promotion that worked. Both are guessing dressed up as strategy.

Attribution is what removes the guessing. It does not have to be sophisticated; it has to answer one question reliably — *where did this subscriber come from?* — because that single answer is what lets you stop funding what does not work and double down on what does. Everything about steering growth depends on being able to see the funnel, and seeing the funnel is exactly what attribution is for.

## The tool: tag your links

The mechanical basis of attribution is the tagged link — a URL that carries a marker telling you where it was posted, so that when someone clicks and eventually subscribes, you can trace them to that source. In its full form this is the world of tracking parameters; in the practical form most creators need, it is often simpler: a distinct link, or a tracked redirect, per channel, so that the source is recorded by *which* link got used.

The important thing is to keep it at a level you will actually maintain. You do not need an enterprise analytics stack; you need to reliably know which door each subscriber walked through. An over-instrumented system you never look at is worse than a simple one you keep, because attribution only pays off when you actually read it and act on it.

## The platform problem, and the trick that survives it

Here is where generic tracking advice fails creators, and why this needs its own guide: **platforms strip tracking parameters, and the subscribe often happens somewhere you cannot see the referrer at all.** A clean parameter you attach to a link can be gone by the time the fan reaches the paywall, so the naive approach quietly stops working and you are back to guessing without realising it.

The durable workaround is to **encode the source in the link itself, not in a parameter attached to it.** If each channel points at its own distinct tracked destination — a different link-in-bio target or a different redirect per source — then which link the fan used tells you the source even after every parameter has been stripped, and even when the final step is invisible to you. It is less elegant than tidy parameters and much harder to break, which in this environment is exactly the trade you want. Build your tracking assuming the parameters will not survive, and it will keep telling you the truth when they do not.

## Measure buyers, not just clicks

The most important distinction in the whole exercise: track through to the **subscribe**, not just the click. A channel can send a flood of clicks and almost no buyers, and if you measure clicks alone it will look like your best source right up until you notice the revenue is not there. Traffic that clicks and traffic that pays are different populations, and the gap between them is usually a [message-match](/resources/growth/message-match) problem — a channel whose promise does not survive the trip to your page.

So measure both, but decide on buyers. Which sources produce subscribers, at what effort, is the number that should drive where your hours go — and it is the prerequisite for allocating time sensibly across channels, the question behind [which subreddits are worth your hours](/resources/growth). Attribution is also what a good [weekly reporting](/resources/growth/weekly-reporting) rhythm surfaces, so that a channel quietly dying, or quietly working, shows up while you can still act on it. Get the instrumentation right, keep it simple enough to actually read, and you trade a pile of activities you hope are working for a funnel you can see and steer — which is the whole of the [growth hub](/resources/growth) in one habit.
