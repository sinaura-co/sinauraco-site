The choice between chat support and full management gets framed, almost always, as a question of *how much service* you want — a little help with the DMs, or someone to run the whole thing. That framing feels natural and it points you at the wrong decision, because the real difference between the two is not how much they do. It is how much control you hand over. This guide is part of our [agency-selection hub](/resources/agency-selection), and it is about reframing the choice around the thing that actually differs: who ends up holding the wheel.

## Scope is the wrong axis

The usual picture is a spectrum of service volume: chat support is the small end (they run your inbox), full management is the large end (they run everything). It is a tidy way to describe the packages and a poor way to make the decision, because "how much do they do" is not the variable that determines what the relationship is actually like. Two arrangements with identical scope can differ enormously in who is in charge, and that difference — not the size of the service — is what you will feel every day and what you should be deciding on.

So set scope aside as the primary axis. It is a description, not a decision. The decision is about control, and control has two parts.

## The real question: who owns the calendar

The **content calendar** — what gets made, when it goes out, and what gets sold when — is the steering wheel of a creator business. Whoever controls it is driving, whatever the service is called. This is the true dividing line between chat support and full management: with chat support, *you* keep the calendar and someone executes the inbox inside the plan you set; with full management, the agency *takes* the calendar and makes those decisions for you. That is a real transfer of power, and it is the thing you are actually choosing between when you pick a tier.

Framed this way, the labels stop mattering and the behaviour starts. A package called "support" that quietly makes your posting and selling decisions has taken the wheel no matter how modest it sounds. A "full" package that leaves the calendar decisions with you has not, whatever it is priced at. So the question to ask any agency is blunt and clarifying: *who decides what gets posted and sold, and when?* The answer tells you who is driving, which is what you needed to know.

## And who touches the money

There is a second axis of control, and it is one you should hold firm on regardless of tier: **who touches the payout.** Control of the calendar is a legitimate thing to hand over as you scale; control of your account and your money is not, and a good agency never needs it. Your account stays in your name and your payouts flow to your bank under your control whether you buy chat support or full management — the whole point of [account access and payouts](/resources/agency-selection/account-access-and-payouts), where a real operation works through co-manager access you grant and can revoke, and never asks for your password.

This matters because control of the calendar and control of the money get bundled together in bad pitches. "Full management" is sometimes sold as *also* taking your login and sitting between you and your payouts — and that is not a higher service tier, it is a control grab wearing one. Keep the two axes separate in your head: you might hand over the calendar as you grow; you should never hand over the account and the money.

## Reframe the buying decision

Once you see it as control rather than scope, the decision gets clearer. Ask not "how much service do I want?" but "**how much control do I actually want to hand over, and is now the time?**" If your bottleneck is the inbox and you still want to steer the business, chat support keeps you in the driver's seat with help exactly where you need it. If operations have genuinely outgrown what you can run alone and you want to step out of the day-to-day, full management takes the wheel — which is the right move when the load has outgrown you and the wrong one when it has not.

That "is now the time" is a stage question, and it is the same one running through [signs you're ready for management](/resources/getting-started/ready-for-management), [what breaks the plateau](/resources/getting-started/breaking-the-ceiling), and the case for why an agency should decline creators who are [too early](/resources/agency-selection/too-small-for-an-agency). Handing over the calendar before operations have outgrown you buys control-loss you did not need; refusing to hand it over long after they have keeps you trapped running a business that is strangling your growth. Decide from your stage and from how much of the wheel you want to hold — and get whichever you choose written into the agreement, because as always the [contract](/resources/agency-selection/reading-the-contract), not the pitch, is the real deal.
